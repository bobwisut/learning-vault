Visual lessons work best when you read them with your eyes first and the text second.

<FadeIn delay={0.1}>
  The goal is not to memorize every term. The goal is to notice one change, explain it,
  then connect that explanation to something you could build or recognize later.
</FadeIn>

Use this loop:

```text
Hook → Concept Map → Demo → Presets → Practice
```

## Concept Map

The concept map is the reading path. It tells you which part of the lesson to
use first, then what to check next.

<ReadingPathDiagram />

## What Each Step Does

**Hook** gives you the effect before the explanation. Ask: what changed?

**Concept map** shows the order of ideas. Ask: what value moves from one step to the next?

**Demo** lets you test cause and effect. Move one control and describe the result.

**Presets** show useful examples. Compare two presets and name the difference.

**Practice** turns the idea into memory. Make a tiny transfer to a new example.

## Three Useful Reading Modes

- **First pass:** skim the headings and play with the visual.
- **Second pass:** read the concept map and explain the chain out loud.
- **Build pass:** use the practice prompt to make a small variation.

## Common Mistakes

- Reading the full page before looking at the demo.
- Moving every control at once, then losing track of what caused the change.
- Treating the diagram as decoration instead of the lesson's main path.
- Skipping the practice prompt, which is where the idea becomes usable.

## Practice

Open one shader lesson. Pick a preset, change one control, and write one sentence:

```text
When I changed ____, the visual changed by ____ because ____.
```
